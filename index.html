Design / Fabrication

    • Apply aerospace concepts through an iterative design-build-test process.
  • Approach:

Turbulence Model Accuracy on a Low-Reynolds-Number E387 Airfoil

CFD / ANSYS
  • Goal:
    • Compare k–ω SST and Transition SST (γ–Reθ) predictions for the E387 airfoil at low Reynolds numbers.
    • Determine which model better represents transitional flow by validating against experimental wind-tunnel data.
  • Approach:
    • Developed a 2D E387 airfoil CFD model in ANSYS Fluent at Re = 200,000 and 300,000.
    • Simulated multiple angles of attack using both turbulence models.
    • Compared lift coefficient, drag coefficient, and lift-to-drag ratio against UIUC experimental data.
    • Quantified prediction errors for each model and operating condition, beyond checking solver convergence.
    • Analysed pressure, skin-friction, and velocity contours to investigate transition, separation, and stall behaviour.
    • Identified limitations of steady RANS modelling at higher angles of attack, where solutions became oscillatory and separation-dominated.
  • Outcome:
    • Found that Transition SST generally agreed better with experimental data in the stable pre-stall region.
    • Achieved lift-prediction errors as low as 0.5% at Re = 200,000 and 1.8% at Re = 300,000 using Transition SST.
    • Found k–ω SST drag-prediction errors above 40% at several Re = 200,000 conditions, showing the value of transition modelling at low Reynolds numbers.
    • Demonstrated that model selection and validation significantly affect the reliability of CFD predictions.

Flow Quality Characterisation & Honeycomb Straightener Evaluation of the AF1300 Wind Tunnel

Experimental Aerodynamics
  • Goal:
    • Characterise the existing flow quality of the AF1300 low-speed wind tunnel.
    • Determine whether low-cost honeycomb flow-straighteners improve flow uniformity, angularity, boundary-layer development, and steadiness.
    • Identify a practical operating envelope for future aerodynamic testing.
  • Approach:
    • Developed a methodology to characterise the empty AF1300 test section.
    • Used traversable Pitot-static measurements and the AFA6/VDAS pressure-acquisition system to collect velocity and pressure data.
    • Measured velocity variation across the test section to assess flow uniformity.
    • Measured near-wall velocity profiles at multiple longitudinal positions to investigate boundary-layer development.
    • Investigated pitch and yaw flow angularity where instrumentation allowed, and recorded pressure over time to assess flow steadiness.
    • Designed stacked and staggered removable honeycomb flow-conditioning concepts in CAD and 3D-printed them for testing without permanent tunnel modifications.
    • Compared both configurations with the uncorrected baseline under consistent conditions.
  • Outcome:
    • The project is ongoing; final experimental results have not yet been established.
    • The methodology will provide a structured characterisation of the AF1300's existing flow quality.
    • Testing will determine whether either honeycomb configuration measurably improves flow over the baseline.
    • The final study will define a usable spatial and operating envelope and recommend flow-conditioning options.
